# Sales Battlecard Generator

## Task

Create a practical sales battlecard from the provided launch materials.

The battlecard should help a seller quickly understand the customer problem, position the product, qualify opportunities, handle common objections, and differentiate the product from the alternatives identified in the source materials.

This is a field-facing sales asset, not a comprehensive product document.

Do not invent competitors, product capabilities, integrations, customer evidence, pricing, performance claims, security certifications, or objection responses that are not supported by the source materials.

If the source materials do not provide enough information to support a claim or response, explicitly identify the gap rather than inventing an answer.

## Writing Objectives

* Optimize for a seller preparing for or participating in a customer conversation.
* Make the most important information easy to scan.
* Lead with customer problems and buying triggers.
* Translate product capabilities into customer value.
* Prioritize differentiation that matters in a buying decision.
* Use concise, conversational language a seller could actually say.
* Avoid generic AI positioning.
* Avoid repeating the same value proposition across sections.
* Distinguish supported facts from assumptions or claims requiring validation.

## Output Structure

Produce a Markdown document with the following sections:

# Sales Battlecard

## At a Glance

Include:

* Product
* Category
* Primary buyer
* Primary user
* Ideal customer profile
* Core customer problem
* Primary value proposition

## 30-Second Pitch

Write a concise pitch a seller could deliver conversationally.

The pitch should explain:

1. The customer problem
2. What the product does
3. Why it matters
4. What makes it different

Do not use jargon or unsupported claims.

## When to Lead With This Product

Identify the customer situations, pain points, or buying signals that should cause a seller to introduce the product.

Use only signals supported by the source materials.

## Discovery Questions

Provide 6–8 questions a seller can use to uncover:

* Current knowledge-discovery process
* Time spent finding information
* Problems with existing search
* Use of public AI tools
* Trust and accuracy concerns
* Security and governance requirements
* Repetitive internal questions
* Knowledge gaps

Questions should encourage conversation rather than solicit yes/no answers.

## Key Messages

Provide exactly three key messages.

For each include:

### [Message]

**Customer problem**

**What to say**

**Why it matters**

**Supporting capabilities**

Keep "What to say" conversational enough that a seller could use the language directly with a prospect.

## Competitive Positioning

Cover only the alternatives supported by the launch materials:

### Traditional Enterprise Search

Include:

* Why customers use it
* Where it falls short
* How to position Atlas Knowledge
* What not to claim

### Public AI Assistants

Include:

* Why customers use them
* Where they fall short
* How to position Atlas Knowledge
* What not to claim

### Manual Knowledge Discovery

Include:

* Why customers rely on it
* Where it falls short
* How to position Atlas Knowledge

Do not create unsupported feature-by-feature comparisons.

## Objection Handling

Identify likely objections that can reasonably be inferred from the approved source materials.

For each use:

### "[Objection]"

**Response**

**Proof/support**

If the source materials do not provide sufficient evidence for a confident response, explicitly state what information the seller needs before making the claim.

Prioritize objections involving:

* Accuracy and trust
* Security and permissions
* Existing enterprise search
* Existing public AI usage
* Quality of underlying company content
* Implementation or administrative concerns

## Audience Plays

Provide a concise sales play for:

* CIO
* IT Administrator
* IT Director
* Information Security
* Business Unit Leader

For each include:

**What they care about**

**Lead with**

**Ask**

**Avoid**

## Proof Points

List only facts and capabilities directly supported by the approved source materials.

Separate them into:

### Approved Proof

Claims sellers can use.

### Proof Still Needed

Evidence that would materially strengthen the sales story but is not available in the source materials.

## Qualification

### Strong Fit

List characteristics indicating a strong potential fit.

### Potential Risks

List conditions that could make adoption or value realization more difficult.

Do not invent formal qualification criteria that are not supported by the source materials.

## Seller Guardrails

Clearly identify:

* Claims sellers should not make
* Capabilities that are out of scope for this release
* Areas where additional validation is required
* Technical terms sellers should be prepared to explain
