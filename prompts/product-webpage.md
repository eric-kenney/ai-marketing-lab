# Product Webpage Generator

## Task

Create launch-ready product webpage copy from the provided launch materials.

The webpage should help a prospective buyer quickly understand:

1. The problem the product solves
2. What the product does
3. Why the product is different
4. The business value
5. Why the buyer should believe the claims
6. What action to take next

This is customer-facing marketing copy, not a product specification or internal messaging document.

Do not invent product capabilities, integrations, customer evidence, pricing, performance claims, security certifications, analyst recognition, or business results.

If the source materials do not provide evidence for a potentially valuable claim, do not use the claim in the customer-facing copy.

## Writing Objectives

* Lead with the customer problem and outcome, not the technology.
* Make the value proposition clear within the hero section.
* Use concise, confident enterprise marketing language.
* Translate capabilities into customer value.
* Make differentiation visible without attacking competitors.
* Avoid generic AI messaging.
* Avoid technical terminology unless it materially strengthens the message.
* Avoid repetition between sections.
* Prefer specific statements over adjectives and superlatives.
* Make headlines communicate an idea rather than merely label a section.
* Write copy that could realistically appear on a B2B software product page.

## Output Structure

Produce a Markdown document using the following structure:

# Product Webpage

## Hero

### Headline

Write one concise headline focused on the primary customer outcome.

### Subheadline

Explain what the product does, who it helps, and the primary differentiation.

### Primary CTA

Provide one concise call to action supported by the launch materials.

Do not invent offers, trials, pricing, or promotions.

## Problem

### Headline

Write a customer-focused headline.

### Copy

Explain the current customer problem and why existing approaches fall short.

Focus on the consequences of fragmented knowledge, document-based search, repetitive questions, and uncertainty about information accuracy.

## Value Proposition

### Headline

Write a clear statement of the product's primary value.

### Copy

Explain how the product changes the customer's experience and why that matters.

## Key Benefits

Create exactly three benefit sections based on the approved messaging pillars.

For each include:

### [Benefit Headline]

**Benefit copy**

Explain the customer outcome.

**How it works**

Connect the outcome to supported product capabilities.

Do not simply repeat feature descriptions.

## How It Works

Explain the product experience in three concise steps.

Each step should include:

### [Step Headline]

A short explanation of what happens and why it matters.

Do not invent implementation details that are not provided in the source materials.

## Enterprise Trust and Governance

### Headline

Write a headline addressing enterprise confidence and control.

### Copy

Explain the supported approach to:

* Approved company content
* Citations and source verification
* Existing permissions and access controls

Do not imply certifications, security capabilities, compliance standards, or governance features that are not specified.

## Why Atlas Knowledge

Explain how Atlas Knowledge differs from the alternatives identified in the source materials:

* Traditional enterprise search
* Public AI assistants
* Manual knowledge discovery

Focus on the customer's experience rather than creating a competitive feature table.

## Who It's For

Provide concise messaging for the primary buyer, primary user, and relevant influencers identified in the source materials.

Do not introduce new personas.

## Final CTA

### Headline

Give the buyer a clear reason to take the next step.

### Copy

Summarize the primary value proposition without simply repeating the hero.

### CTA

Provide one concise action.

## Claims Check

This section is for internal review and should not be treated as customer-facing webpage copy.

### Claims Used

List the meaningful product claims made in the webpage and identify the source material supporting each one.

### Claims Avoided

List potentially attractive claims you deliberately did not use because the source materials do not provide sufficient evidence.
