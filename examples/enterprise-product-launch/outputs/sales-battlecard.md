# Sales Battlecard

## At a Glance

- Product: Atlas Knowledge (v1.0)
- Category: Enterprise AI Knowledge Platform
- Primary buyer: CIO
- Primary user: IT Administrator
- Ideal customer profile: Mid-market and enterprise organizations (500–10,000 employees) with distributed teams, significant internal documentation, and multiple business systems where employees struggle to locate trusted information efficiently.
- Core customer problem: Employees spend too much time hunting across tools and reading documents to find current, trustworthy answers. Traditional search returns documents, not answers. Public AI assistants don’t access internal content or authoritative sources.
- Primary value proposition: Give employees accurate, cited answers from across approved company content—through natural language—while respecting existing permissions.

## 30-Second Pitch

Your teams know the information exists, but they burn time figuring out where it lives and whether it’s current. Atlas Knowledge connects to your existing systems and gives employees trusted, cited answers in plain language, grounded in approved company content and gated by existing permissions. The result: less searching, fewer repeat questions, and faster decisions. Unlike public AI assistants or legacy search, Atlas Knowledge cites sources and works across your tools without moving content.

## When to Lead With This Product

- Employees ask the same internal questions repeatedly despite existing documentation.
- Current enterprise search returns long lists of documents instead of concise answers.
- Teams rely on public AI tools but don’t trust the results or can’t use internal content.
- Onboarding takes too long because new hires can’t find policies, processes, or product info.
- Knowledge is spread across document repositories, collaboration tools, CRM, and project systems.
- Leadership wants AI that respects existing permissions and provides verifiable citations.
- There’s interest in understanding knowledge gaps (e.g., unanswered questions, search patterns).

## Discovery Questions

- Walk me through how an employee finds an answer today. Where do they look first, and what typically slows them down?
- How much time do team members spend reading through multiple documents to confirm an answer is current?
- What are the most common internal questions your help channels or ops teams see over and over?
- Are employees using public AI tools to summarize or find information? In what situations do they avoid using them?
- When people do find an answer, how do they verify it’s the authoritative, up-to-date source?
- Which systems hold your most important internal knowledge (docs, collaboration platforms, CRM, project tools)? Where are permissions most complex?
- How do you track unanswered searches or content gaps today? Who owns keeping content current?
- What security or governance requirements must any AI-driven knowledge product meet in your environment?

## Key Messages

### Trusted, cited answers employees can verify

- Customer problem: People don’t know if the information they find is accurate or current.
- What to say: Every answer includes citations to the original, approved source so employees can check the details and move forward with confidence.
- Why it matters: Trust drives adoption. Source-backed answers reduce rework and second-guessing.
- Supporting capabilities: Retrieval-augmented generation grounded in approved content; responses include citations to original sources.

### One place to ask across your tools—without moving content

- Customer problem: Knowledge is scattered across multiple systems, forcing employees to search each one.
- What to say: Ask a question in natural language and Atlas Knowledge pulls from the systems you already use—document repositories, collaboration tools, CRM, and project platforms.
- Why it matters: Employees get to the right information faster without changing where they work or duplicating content.
- Supporting capabilities: Connectors to enterprise knowledge sources; AI-powered semantic search.

### Governed access that mirrors what you already enforce

- Customer problem: AI tools that ignore permissions create risk and stall adoption.
- What to say: Atlas Knowledge respects your existing user permissions and access controls—people only see what they’re authorized to see.
- Why it matters: Reduces security risk and accelerates approval from IT and InfoSec.
- Supporting capabilities: Permission-aware retrieval and response generation; administrative controls.

## Competitive Positioning

### Traditional Enterprise Search

- Why customers use it:
  - Centralized way to index content and make it discoverable.
  - Familiar experience for browsing documents.
- Where it falls short:
  - Returns document lists, not concise answers.
  - Employees still have to read multiple sources to verify accuracy and recency.
- How to position Atlas Knowledge:
  - “Instead of scanning documents, employees get a cited answer grounded in approved content—so they can verify and move on.”
  - Emphasize natural language questions, cited responses, and permission-aware results.
- What not to claim:
  - Do not claim higher accuracy benchmarks or faster performance without evidence.
  - Do not claim feature parity with specific search vendors not listed in materials.

### Public AI Assistants

- Why customers use them:
  - Quick summaries and natural language Q&A.
  - Easy to try for ad-hoc questions.
- Where they fall short:
  - Lack access to internal content.
  - Often fail to identify authoritative sources.
- How to position Atlas Knowledge:
  - “Same conversational ease, but answers are grounded in your approved content and include citations—while respecting existing permissions.”
- What not to claim:
  - Do not imply public assistants never provide correct answers.
  - Do not claim unique security certifications or data handling beyond ‘respects existing permissions.’

### Manual Knowledge Discovery

- Why customers rely on it:
  - Asking coworkers, digging through threads, and hunting in multiple tools.
  - Institutional knowledge hasn’t been fully documented or unified.
- Where it falls short:
  - Time-consuming, error-prone, and leads to repeated questions.
- How to position Atlas Knowledge:
  - “Ask once in natural language, get a trusted, cited answer from your systems—reducing interruptions and onboarding time.”

- Response:

## Audience Plays

### CIO
- What they care about: Workforce productivity, responsible AI adoption, governance.
- Lead with: Cited, permission-aware answers across existing systems; reduced time spent searching; unified knowledge access.
- Ask: Where do teams lose the most time finding information? Which functions suffer most from repeated questions?
- Avoid: ROI percentages or productivity claims not in evidence; implying AI replaces judgment.

### IT Administrator
- What they care about: Minimal admin overhead, using existing permissions, no content migration.
- Lead with: Connectors to current systems; respects existing access controls; analytics to improve content.
- Ask: Which repositories and collaboration tools are in scope? How are permissions modeled today?
- Avoid: Promising content authoring, workflow automation, or customer-facing use cases (out of scope).

### IT Director
- What they care about: Integration coverage, deployment approach, user adoption, governance.
- Lead with: Natural language answers with citations; permission-aware access; analytics for unanswered questions.
- Ask: Top systems to connect first; current pain with search accuracy and trust; content ownership model.
- Avoid: Claims about performance benchmarks or custom model training.

### Information Security
- What they care about: Access control, data governance, auditability.
- Lead with: Honors existing permissions; answers grounded in approved sources users can verify.
- Ask: Any additional governance requirements beyond existing access controls? Review processes for sensitive content?
- Avoid: Claiming security certifications, data residency controls, or compliance frameworks not provided in materials.

### Business Unit Leader
- What they care about: Faster onboarding, fewer interruptions, reliable answers.
- Lead with: Ask in plain language, get a cited answer from your internal sources; reduces repeated questions to your team.
- Ask: What questions slow your team down most? Where do new hires struggle to find information?
- Avoid: Promising external/customer-facing knowledge or task automation.

## Seller Guardrails

- Claims not to make:
  - No “best,” “most accurate,” or performance/savings claims without evidence.
  - No promises about specific security certifications, data residency, or compliance frameworks not provided.
  - No claims of replacing human judgment.
- Capabilities out of scope for v1.0:
  - Content creation/authoring.
  - Workflow automation or task execution.
  - External/customer-facing knowledge experiences.
  - Custom model training or fine-tuning.
  - Industry-specific compliance packages.
- Areas requiring validation:
  - Exact integrations in scope for the prospect.
  - Deployment model, timeline, and administrative requirements.
  - Logging/audit details and any advanced governance features.
  - Pricing and packaging (not provided in materials).
- Technical terms to explain:
  - Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG): generating answers grounded in retrieved, approved content.
  - Semantic search: understanding intent in natural language queries.
  - Citations: links back to the original source content.
  - Permissions/access controls: honoring existing user authorization in connected systems.
  - Knowledge gap analytics: insights on unanswered questions and content needs.
